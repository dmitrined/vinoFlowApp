In winemaking, precision in measurements plays a crucial role. One of the most important tools of the German oenological school is the **Troost Table** (compiled by Gerhard Troost).

It allows for an accurate correlation of must density (expressed in Oechsle degrees) with sugar content and potential alcohol.

## What is Oechsle (°Oe)?

The Oechsle degree is a unit of measurement for grape must density, indicating how many grams one liter of must is heavier than one liter of water at 20°C.

For example, must with a density of 1080 g/L has $1080 - 1000 = 80$ °Oe.

<Note type="info">
The °Oe value is not directly equal to grams of sugar. The must contains other soluble substances (acids, pectins, minerals) that also affect density.
</Note>
